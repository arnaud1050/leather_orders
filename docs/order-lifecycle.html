Atelier Order Book

The life of an order

Every order moves through a handful of stages, from first conversation to finished piece in the client's hands. This page shows the whole path — what each stage means, and what can happen next from each one.

The whole path at a glance

What each stage means

Tentative

Not yet committed

The conversation is still going. Details aren't settled, the deposit hasn't arrived, and nothing has been promised. It sits on the schedule anyway, so you can see whether you'd actually have room for it.

Next: becomes Confirmed once the deposit is taken — or, if it never goes ahead, it's the one stage that can be removed completely.

Confirmed → In progress

Underway

The order is real: agreed, deposited, and holding a slot on the schedule. Until its start date arrives it reads Confirmed; on the start date it renames itself to In progress. You don't have to do anything to make that happen.

Next: Ready for pickup when the work is done. Can be cancelled, but no longer removed.

Ready for pickup

Underway

Finished and waiting for the client to collect it. The piece is done; the order isn't closed until it's actually in their hands.

Next: Delivered when the client collects it. Can still be cancelled if something goes wrong at the last moment.

Delivered

Closed

The client has the piece. Nothing more is owed. It keeps its place on the schedule — those weeks were genuinely spent on it — and simply scrolls out of view as time moves on. It stays in the orders list and on the client's record for good.

Next: nothing — this is the end of the road.

Cancelled

Closed

An order that was real but was called off — by the client, or by you. It disappears from the schedule so it stops taking up space you could be using, but it isn't erased.

Next: nothing — this is the end of the road.

Two things worth knowing

Marking an order as rush

Rush isn't a stage of its own — it's a flag you add on top of one, so a rush order still moves through the same path as everything else. Its bar turns red on the schedule, and the schedule can be sorted to float rush orders to the top.

It applies only while an order is Confirmed or In progress — work that's on its way to the bench, or on it. Once a piece is finished and waiting to be collected there's nothing left for the studio to hurry, so moving an order to Ready for pickup takes the flag off by itself.

Until then you can add or remove it whenever you like.

Removing vs. cancelling

These sound similar and aren't. Removing is only possible while an order is still Tentative, and only while nothing is attached to it — an enquiry that came to nothing, or something entered by mistake. It disappears entirely, with no trace left.

Once an order is real, it can never be removed — only cancelled. You'll be asked to say why, and that reason is saved onto the order's notes, so months later you can still see what happened and why.

Cancelling never touches your stock. Materials drawn against an order stay exactly as they are.

Quick answers

QuestionAnswer
Which orders show on the schedule? Everything except cancelled orders. A cancelled order disappears from the schedule the moment you call it off, because it stops needing your time. A delivered one stays on its dates — it did take that time, and hiding it would leave a gap in a week you were actually busy. It scrolls out of view naturally as the weeks move on.
Why show tentative orders at all? So you can see what might land before you agree to it. A month that looks empty but has four tentative orders in it isn't an empty month.
Can an order ever go backwards? No. An order only moves forward, or gets cancelled. If something was marked too early, correct it by editing the order rather than by moving it back a stage.
Where do closed orders go? Nowhere. Delivered and cancelled orders both stay in the orders list and on the client's record for good — a cancelled one shown greyed out and struck through, so it reads as closed at a glance.
What if a tentative order has a deposit on it? Then it can't be removed, even while tentative. Once money is attached to an order, there's a record to keep.
What if I've already cut materials for it? Then it can't be removed either, until you clear those materials yourself. Only you know whether the leather went back on the shelf or was used up on a prototype — so the app won't guess, and won't quietly put stock back. Clear the materials on the order's Materials tab, deciding one at a time, and the option to remove it appears. Cancelling instead leaves everything as it is.
