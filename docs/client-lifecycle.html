The life of a client

Somebody writes in. Months later they're a name you know, with three orders behind them. This page shows what happens in between — where an enquiry sits before it's anybody, what makes it a client, and what the app does by itself when mail arrives.

The whole path at a glance

What each stage means

Enquiry

Not anybody yet

Mail from an address the app doesn't recognise, waiting in the lead inbox. It could be a customer, a supplier, or spam — nobody has looked yet, so the app doesn't guess. An enquiry is a conversation, not a person: nothing has been added to your client list.

Next: becomes a client when you convert it, or when a rule you set up converts it for you. Or it's hidden, or moved to your mailbox's Trash.

On the client list

A real record

A person with a record of their own: name, email, phone, address, how they heard about you, and your notes. Most arrive with no orders — an enquiry you decided was genuine, or somebody who walked in and gave you their details.

Next: their first order. There's no deadline on that, and no stage they fall out of if it never comes.

Has ordered

Doing business

The same record, now with work attached. Their lifetime value starts counting, they appear in the takings, and their orders run their own course — see The life of an order for that half.

Next: a second order marks them Returning, shown as a star beside their name.

Hidden

Off the list

Still a client in every way that matters — you've just taken them off the list you read every day. Their own page still works, and everything they ever did is still on it.

Next: back on the list whenever you say so — or by themselves, the moment they email you again.

What happens when mail arrives

Every one of these is the app deciding where an incoming message belongs. The rules are always the same; what differs is whether the address is one it already knows, and whether you've told it anything about that sender in advance.

A stranger writes in

The address matches nobody on your client list, so the conversation goes to the lead inbox and the grey 1 appears beside Clients. It stays there until you do something with it — reading it doesn't clear the count, because reading an enquiry isn't answering it.

You convert it by hand

The ordinary case, and the one you'll use most.

Their name is already filled in for you, worked out from how they signed the mail — or, failing that, from their email address, so "marie.alarie@…" arrives as Marie Alarie. Correct it if it's wrong; what you submit always wins.

The conversation moves onto their new record, and so does any other unclaimed mail from the same address — a first message and three follow-ups all end up in one place.

Your website's contact form submits

Only if you've set up a rule for it.

These arrive from your website provider's address rather than the customer's, so without a rule every submission looks like the same sender. Tell the app that address is your contact form, and each one becomes a client automatically, announced with a purple badge on the Clients link.

Tell it what your form's labels are called — Name, Phone, How did you hear about us? — and those answers land in the matching fields instead of sitting in a wall of text. Unlabelled, the rule still works; it just produces clients named after your website provider.

…and that person is already on your list

Nothing is duplicated. The conversation goes onto the record you already have, and anything the form filled in is used only to fill blanks — a phone number you corrected by hand is never overwritten by one they retyped into a web form. No new client, so no "new client" badge; the unread-mail badge is what tells you they wrote.

A newsletter, or a supplier you'll never file

Only if you've set up a rule for it.

Tell the app to hide that sender and it stops reaching the lead inbox at all. The mail is kept and still readable, and your real mailbox is untouched — the rule only decides what this app shows you.

Unlike a conversation you hide, a rule-hidden one doesn't come back when the sender writes again. That's the point: a newsletter arrives every week, and a rule that un-hides it every week hasn't worked. Remove the rule to undo it.

An existing client writes

No lead, no decision to make. The address is recognised, so the conversation attaches to their record straight away and a purple 2 counts the messages you haven't read — on the Clients link, beside their name on the list, and on their own Emails tab.

It counts messages, not conversations, so a reply three months into an old thread still reaches you. It clears only by opening the conversation — looking at the list isn't reading what they said.

Mail that arrived before they existed

Add a client, or correct an email address, and the app goes back through the unclaimed mail and attaches anything from that address. You don't have to wait for the person to write again.

A hidden client writes

They come back onto the client list by themselves. Someone getting back in touch is exactly the moment you'd want them there, and it saves you remembering that you hid them.

Three things worth knowing

Hiding a client

There is no way to delete a client, and there won't be. Their orders point at them, so do their invoices and every email they ever sent — deleting the record would leave all of it referring to nobody.

Hiding is about the list, and only the list. They come off the client list and out of the picker when you start a new order. That's all it does.

Their orders stay exactly where they are — on the schedule, in the orders list, in your revenue and tax figures — and their name still shows on every one of them. An order is time you actually spent and money you were actually owed, and tidying a contact list doesn't get to retract that.

Use it for an enquiry that went nowhere, a duplicate, or somebody you've stopped dealing with. Nothing is lost, and you can put them back at any time.

The two badges beside Clients

The grey one counts enquiries waiting for you to decide something. It's deliberately colourless: the app has no idea whether that message is a customer or spam, and a louder colour would claim more than it knows.

The purple one means something worth knowing happened — unread mail from a client, or a client a rule added while you weren't looking. Purple clears by looking; grey clears by doing.

They never both count the same person, and both can show at once: triaging a stranger and replying to a client are different work with different answers.

An enquiry is not a client

This is the distinction the whole page rests on. Mail from a stranger sits in the lead inbox as a conversation. Nothing is on your client list until somebody — you, or a rule you wrote — says it should be.

That's on purpose. An inbox is newsletters, suppliers and spam; a client record per sender would make the list useless within a month. The one exception is a sender you've explicitly named, which is you making the decision once instead of every time.

Going the other way is a matter of fact, not of judgement — a client counts as having ordered because an order exists, and as returning because a second one does. There's no button for either.

Quick answers

QuestionAnswer
Can I delete a client? No — hide them instead. Orders, invoices and email conversations all refer to a client, so removing the record would leave those pointing at nothing. Hiding gets you the same tidy list with none of that.
Does hiding a client hide their orders? No, and this is the one thing worth being certain about. Their orders stay on the schedule, in the orders list, and in every figure on the Analytics page. Hiding answers "do I still deal with this person"; it isn't a way to take work off the books.
Can I place an order for a hidden client? Not without putting them back on the list first — they aren't offered in the client picker on a new order. Show them again and they're there. Hiding somebody you're about to do work for is the contradiction the app is quietly pointing out.
Do hidden clients still count in my figures? Yes. Revenue, lifetime value, top clients, "how did you hear about us" — all unchanged. Leaving a hidden client out would mean last year's takings changed because you tidied a list this morning.
What's the difference between hiding an enquiry and hiding a client? An enquiry is a conversation, and hiding it takes it out of the lead inbox. A client is a person, and hiding them takes them off the client list. Both are reversible, both keep everything, and both undo themselves when the person writes to you again.
Somebody filled in the contact form twice. Do I get two records? Not if they used the same email address both times — the second one attaches to the record that exists. A different address does produce a second record, because two people share a name far more often than they share an inbox, and merging two clients isn't something an unattended sync should be able to do.
Why is there no "client since" date? Because their first order already says it, and better. A date on the record would tell you when somebody typed them in; the order tells you when you started working for them.
Can a client go backwards? Only in the sense that any of it can be edited. Nothing expires: a client with one order from four years ago is still a client, and still shows on the list. The only thing that ever removes somebody from view is you hiding them.
