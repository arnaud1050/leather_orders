Labour and overhead

{{ notice_slot(notice, 'costs') }}

What an hour of your time is worth, and what running the studio adds on top. These decide the margin on every order's Costs tab and the price its calculator suggests. None of it is ever shown to a client.

{{ icon_info() }}What counts as overhead, and how to pick the percentage {{ icon_chevron_down() }}

Overhead is what it costs to keep the studio running that can't be pinned to one order: rent, power and heating, tools wearing out (blades, punches, machine servicing), small supplies nobody logs per order (thread, glue, edge paint, packaging), insurance, software, card fees. If no order carries a share of it, every order looks more profitable than it is. It's added as a percentage on top of each order's materials and labour.

To pick the percentage, divide a year of those costs by a year of materials and labour across all your orders: $9,000 of shop costs against $60,000 of work is 15%. A rough figure is fine. If your hourly rate was already set to cover rent and tools rather than to pay you, set overhead to 0 so it isn't counted twice.

Time already logged keeps the rate it was logged at, so changing the rate only affects time logged from now on. Overhead and the target margin are read fresh everywhere, past orders included.
